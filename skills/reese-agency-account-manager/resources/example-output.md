# Example Output — Reese, Agency Account Manager

**Client scenario:** Reese is the account manager for Brightline Media (a 12-person social + paid media agency) on the Northfield Outdoor Supply account — a mid-size outdoor gear retailer paying $9,000/month for paid social and Meta ads management. The relationship is 8 months in. This period had one problem (a paid campaign underperformed badly for 3 weeks due to a Meta account flagging issue Brightline caused by uploading a policy-violating creative) and one strong win (email-attributed revenue from a new retargeting flow hit $41,000 in the period, against a $9,000 monthly fee). Contract renews in 75 days. Reese needs: a status update covering both, a bad-news delivery for the campaign issue, and a renewal-prep note.

---

## Monthly Status Update (sent to Northfield's marketing director, Casey Alvarez)

**Subject: Northfield — March Results & One Thing to Flag**

Hi Casey,

Leading with the headline: the new retargeting flow we launched in February drove $41,000 in attributed revenue this month — against your $9,000 monthly spend with us, that's the strongest ROAS this account has seen since we started. I'll break down why below.

I also need to flag something that cost us performance for about 3 weeks this month, and I want to be direct about it rather than bury it in the good news.

**The win: retargeting flow**
The abandoned-cart and browse-retargeting sequence we built in February started serving in early March. It's now driving 22% of total attributed revenue from paid social, at a cost per acquisition roughly 40% lower than cold prospecting campaigns. Recommend we expand this into a third segment (past purchasers, 90+ days) next month — more detail below.

**The issue: Meta account flag, March 3-24**
On March 3rd, one of our creative uploads triggered a Meta policy flag (an image was interpreted as making an unsubstantiated health claim about one of your recovery-gear products — not something either of us caught in review). This restricted delivery on 2 of your 5 active campaigns for roughly 3 weeks while we appealed. Those 2 campaigns underperformed their monthly targets by approximately 30% as a result. We've since had the restriction lifted, tightened our internal creative review to catch this category of claim before upload, and both campaigns are back to full delivery as of March 25th.

**Where things stand overall:** despite the 3-week restriction, total attributed revenue for the month still landed at $41,000, driven by the retargeting flow holding up performance while the 2 restricted campaigns recovered. Full performance detail is in the dashboard as always — happy to walk through it live if useful.

Reese

---

## Bad-News Script (used as the basis for a call placed the day the flag happened, before it appeared in the monthly report)

"Casey, wanted to get you on a quick call rather than email this. Two of your campaigns just got restricted by Meta — one of our creative uploads got flagged for what Meta read as a health claim on the recovery sleeve product. That wasn't intentional and it's on us for not catching it before it went live. Practically, this means those 2 campaigns stop delivering until we get the flag resolved, which typically takes 1-3 weeks. Here's what we're doing right now: filing the appeal today, pausing that specific creative across all campaigns so nothing else gets flagged alongside it, and shifting budget from the restricted campaigns into the retargeting flow so you're not losing spend efficiency while we wait. I'll update you within 48 hours on the appeal status, and I'll flag this transparently in the monthly report too — no surprises."

---

## Upsell Conversation Framework (for expanding the retargeting flow, tied to the proof above)

**Opening, anchored to the result already delivered:** "The retargeting flow drove $41,000 this month at a CPA about 40% below cold prospecting — that's with 2 segments live. There's a third segment we haven't touched yet: customers who purchased 90+ days ago and haven't returned. Given what the first 2 segments are doing, I'd expect this one to add another $8,000-12,000 in monthly attributed revenue at a similar efficiency."

**The ask:** "This would mean building one more flow — about 6 hours of setup — and no change to your monthly retainer, since it fits inside our existing scope. If it performs the way the first two did, it's worth talking about whether retargeting overall deserves a bigger share of the monthly budget next quarter."

---

## Renewal-Prep Note (internal, written by Reese for the Brightline team, 75 days out from contract end)

"Northfield renews in 75 days. Health signals are strong: Casey has been responsive within a day on every ask this quarter, the retargeting win gives us a concrete number to renew around, and there's been no late payment in 8 months. The one risk is the March Meta flag — Casey didn't push back hard, but I want to make sure the renewal conversation acknowledges it was handled well rather than hoping it's forgotten. Plan: start the renewal conversation at day 60 (mid-April), lead with year-to-date attributed revenue and the retargeting expansion result, and propose a 12-month renewal with a modest scope increase (add the third retargeting segment permanently) rather than a flat renewal — gives Casey a reason to say yes beyond inertia."
