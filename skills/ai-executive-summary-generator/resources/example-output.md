# Worked Example: AI Executive Summary Generator

Two complete outputs, start to finish, for two real-shaped requests. Each one
includes the condensed source material first, so the compression is visible and
the reader can check that nothing was added.

The first source contains no recommendation, and the summary says so instead of
manufacturing one. The second source contradicts itself on a key number, and the
summary preserves the contradiction rather than picking the more flattering
figure and moving on.

Every organisation, person and figure below is fictional. The Assumptions
section at the end lists every number as an assumption.

---

## Scenario 1: A status report with no ask in it

### The source material (condensed from an 11 page document)

> **"Fieldstone Grid Modernisation Programme, Board Update, September 2026"**
>
> An eleven page programme update covering the eighteen month substation
> automation rollout. Contents in order: a two page history of why the programme
> was approved, the original business case, a description of the governance
> structure and who sits on the steering group, then the status.
>
> Status as reported: 34 of 61 substations are cut over, against a plan of 41 by
> this point. The delay is attributed to a single vendor, Calder Controls, whose
> relay units arrived an average of nine weeks late across three shipments. The
> programme has spent 12.4 million of an approved 18.9 million. Average cutover
> duration has improved from 14 days on the first ten sites to 8 days on the
> most recent ten. The document notes that the remaining 27 sites include the
> eleven highest-complexity sites, which were deliberately sequenced last, and
> that the 8 day average is therefore unlikely to hold.
>
> Two risks are named: Calder Controls has not confirmed delivery dates beyond
> January 2027, and the programme's lead automation engineer resigned in August
> with no replacement appointed.
>
> The document ends: "The programme team will continue to monitor vendor
> performance and report at the next quarterly board meeting."

### What had to be decided

1. **There is no recommendation, no ask and no decision point in the source.**
   The document ends by saying the team will keep monitoring. The summary states
   that plainly under Recommendation or Ask rather than inventing one. A board
   reading a manufactured recommendation would assume the programme team made it.
2. **The 8 day cutover average could not be presented as a simple improvement.**
   The source itself says the remaining sites are the complex ones and the
   average will not hold. Reporting the improvement without that qualifier would
   be the single most misleading thing this summary could do.
3. **The two pages of programme history and the governance section were cut
   entirely.** A board that approved the programme knows why it approved it.
4. **Figures preserved exactly:** 34 of 61, plan of 41, nine weeks, 12.4 million
   of 18.9 million, 14 days to 8 days, 27 remaining, 11 high complexity, January
   2027. None rounded, none generalised.
5. **The engineer's resignation is under Risks, not Key Points.** It is a named
   risk in the source and it is unmitigated, which is exactly what a risk section
   is for.

### Executive Summary: Fieldstone Grid Modernisation Programme, September 2026

#### Overview

The substation automation rollout is behind plan by seven sites, with the delay
traced to one vendor. Spending is broadly proportionate to progress. The
programme has not asked the board for anything.

#### Key Points

- 34 of 61 substations are cut over, against a plan of 41 at this point
- The shortfall is attributed to Calder Controls relay units arriving an average
  of nine weeks late across three shipments
- Cutover duration has fallen from a 14 day average on the first ten sites to 8
  days on the most recent ten
- The programme states the 8 day average will not hold, because the 11
  highest-complexity sites were deliberately sequenced into the remaining 27
- Calder Controls has not confirmed any delivery date beyond January 2027
- The lead automation engineer resigned in August and has not been replaced

#### Numbers That Matter

- Sites complete: 34 of 61 (plan: 41)
- Sites remaining: 27, of which 11 are highest complexity
- Spend: 12.4 million of an approved 18.9 million
- Vendor delay: nine weeks average, across three shipments
- Cutover duration: 14 days average (first ten sites), 8 days (most recent ten)

#### Recommendation or Ask

**None was stated in the source material.** The document closes by saying the
programme team will continue to monitor vendor performance and report at the
next quarterly board meeting. No decision, approval, funding request or
escalation is requested anywhere in the eleven pages.

#### Risks or Open Questions

- **Vendor delivery beyond January 2027 is unconfirmed.** The delay that caused
  the current shortfall has no stated end date.
- **No lead automation engineer since August.** The source names the resignation
  and does not describe a replacement plan, interim cover, or the effect on the
  remaining schedule.
- **The remaining sites are the hard ones.** The source states this and states
  that the improved cutover rate will not hold, but gives no revised forecast
  completion date.

## Scenario 2: A source that contradicts itself

### The source material (condensed from a 6 page document plus an appendix)

> **"Northreach Learning: Pilot Results, Digital Numeracy Programme"**
>
> A six page evaluation of a two-term pilot across nine partner schools, plus a
> data appendix.
>
> Page 2 states the pilot reached "over 2,400 learners." The appendix table on
> page 6 lists enrolment by school and totals 1,932. Neither figure is
> reconciled against the other and the difference is not mentioned anywhere.
>
> The document reports that 71 percent of participating learners improved their
> assessed numeracy band by at least one level. A footnote states this is
> calculated on learners who sat both the entry and exit assessment. The number
> who sat both is not given anywhere in the document or the appendix.
>
> Cost is reported as 214,000 for the pilot period. A separate line states the
> per-learner cost as "approximately 89," which is consistent with the 2,400
> figure and not with 1,932.
>
> Two of the nine schools withdrew before the second term. The document says
> their data is "excluded from the results above" but does not say whether their
> learners are inside or outside the 2,400 or the 1,932.
>
> The document recommends extending the programme to 25 schools for a full
> academic year, at an estimated 615,000, and asks the board to approve this at
> the November meeting.

### What had to be decided

1. **The enrolment figure contradicts itself and both numbers are reported.**
   2,400 and 1,932 both appear in the source. The summary carries both, names
   the difference as 468, and states that the source does not reconcile them.
   Picking one would be an editorial judgement the summarist is not entitled to
   make, and picking the larger one flatters the ask.
2. **The 71 percent headline is reported with its denominator gap attached.**
   The source discloses that the figure covers only learners who sat both
   assessments and then never says how many that is. Reporting 71 percent
   without that is the difference between a summary and a press release.
3. **The per-learner cost is shown as inconsistent, with both derivations.** The
   source's own "approximately 89" implies the 2,400 denominator. Against 1,932
   the same 214,000 gives a materially higher figure. Both are shown.
4. **The withdrawn schools are a named gap, not a footnote.** Whether their
   learners are inside the enrolment figures determines which enrolment number
   is even plausible, so it sits in Open Questions.
5. **There IS a clear ask here**, unlike Scenario 1, and it is reported exactly
   as stated: 25 schools, full academic year, 615,000, November board meeting.
6. **No figure was recomputed and presented as fact.** Where a derived figure
   appears below, it is labelled as derived and the arithmetic is shown.

### Executive Summary: Northreach Learning Digital Numeracy Pilot

#### Overview

A two-term pilot across nine partner schools reports a 71 percent improvement
rate and asks the board to approve a 615,000 expansion to 25 schools in
November. The evaluation reports two different enrolment totals that it does not
reconcile, and the improvement rate is calculated on a population the document
never sizes.

#### Key Points

- The pilot ran two terms across nine partner schools; two of the nine withdrew
  before the second term
- The document states enrolment as "over 2,400 learners" on page 2 and as 1,932
  in the appendix total on page 6, a difference of 468, unreconciled
- 71 percent of participating learners improved their assessed numeracy band by
  at least one level
- That 71 percent covers only learners who sat both entry and exit assessments,
  a number the document does not state anywhere
- Reported pilot cost is 214,000
- The stated per-learner cost of approximately 89 is consistent with the 2,400
  enrolment figure and not with the 1,932 figure
- The recommendation is to extend to 25 schools for a full academic year at an
  estimated 615,000, for approval at the November board meeting

#### Numbers That Matter

- Enrolment: "over 2,400" (page 2) or 1,932 (appendix, page 6). Difference: 468
- Improvement rate: 71 percent, on an unstated denominator
- Schools: 9 in the pilot, 2 withdrew, 25 proposed
- Pilot cost: 214,000
- Stated per-learner cost: approximately 89
- Derived per-learner cost against the appendix enrolment: 214,000 divided by
  1,932 is approximately 110.77. This figure is derived here for comparison and
  does not appear in the source
- Proposed expansion cost: 615,000

#### Recommendation or Ask

Extend the programme from 9 to 25 schools for a full academic year at an
estimated cost of 615,000, for board approval at the November meeting. This is
stated explicitly in the source.

#### Risks or Open Questions

- **Which enrolment figure is correct?** The proposal's cost per learner, and
  therefore the value case for a 615,000 expansion, moves by roughly a quarter
  depending on which number is used. The source does not reconcile them.
- **How many learners sat both assessments?** The 71 percent headline rests on
  this number and it appears nowhere in the document or the appendix. If it is a
  small fraction of enrolment, the headline does not support the expansion case.
- **Are the two withdrawn schools' learners inside or outside the enrolment
  figures?** The source says their results are excluded from the improvement
  rate but does not say whether their enrolment is excluded too. This may be the
  explanation for the 468 gap, or it may be unrelated.
- **Does the 615,000 estimate scale from the pilot's actual cost base?** The
  source gives no breakdown of the 214,000 and no assumptions behind the
  615,000, so the two cannot be compared.

#### Note on this summary

Three of the four open questions above are gaps in the source, not analysis
added here. Nothing has been resolved, averaged, or chosen between. The only
figure computed in this summary is the derived per-learner cost, which is
labelled as derived and shown with its arithmetic.

## Assumptions used in this example

Every figure above is an assumption made to build this worked example. None of
it is real.

- Fieldstone Grid Modernisation Programme, Calder Controls, Northreach Learning
  and every person and school implied are fictional.
- **Scenario 1 assumed figures:** 34 of 61 substations complete against a plan
  of 41; nine week average vendor delay across three shipments; 12.4 million
  spent of 18.9 million approved; cutover duration 14 days average on the first
  ten sites and 8 days on the most recent ten; 27 sites remaining of which 11
  are highest complexity; no confirmed vendor delivery dates beyond January
  2027; lead automation engineer resigned August 2026; source document of eleven
  pages.
- **Scenario 2 assumed figures:** "over 2,400 learners" on page 2 and 1,932 in
  the appendix; 71 percent improved by at least one numeracy band; pilot cost
  214,000; stated per-learner cost approximately 89; nine partner schools with
  two withdrawing; proposed expansion to 25 schools at 615,000; six page
  document plus appendix.
- **Scenario 2 derived figure:** 214,000 divided by 1,932 is approximately
  110.77. This is arithmetic performed on two figures the fictional source
  supplies, shown for comparison and labelled as derived in the summary itself.
- No real programme, evaluation, education outcome, cost benchmark or statistic
  is referenced anywhere in this example. Both source documents are inventions
  written to exercise the two cases that matter: a source with no ask, and a
  source that contradicts itself.
