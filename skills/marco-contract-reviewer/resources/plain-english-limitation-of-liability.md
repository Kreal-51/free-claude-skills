# Plain-English Clause Explanation — Limitation of Liability

**Original clause** (Section 9.1, same contract):
> *"IN NO EVENT SHALL VENDOR'S AGGREGATE LIABILITY ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS
> AGREEMENT EXCEED THE TOTAL FEES PAID BY CUSTOMER IN THE TWELVE (12) MONTHS PRECEDING
> THE EVENT GIVING RISE TO THE CLAIM. IN NO EVENT SHALL VENDOR BE LIABLE FOR ANY
> INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES."*

**Marco's plain-English explanation:**

"In practice, this means: no matter how badly Northgate's platform fails you — even if
it causes a full operational outage, a data loss event, or a compliance breach that costs
Bellcrest real money — the absolute most you can recover from them is whatever you paid
them in the prior 12 months. On a $184,000 ACV deal, that's a hard ceiling of $184,000,
regardless of actual damages.

The second sentence is just as important: Northgate cannot be held liable for
'consequential damages' — which is legal shorthand for the downstream business losses
that are usually the *real* cost of a vendor failure (lost sales, reputational harm,
cost of an emergency vendor switch, regulatory fines). Those categories are excluded
entirely, not just capped.

This is a standard clause structure — most vendors use exactly this language, and courts
generally enforce it. The issue isn't that the clause exists; it's that it's one-sided
here (see Critical Issue #1 above) and the cap multiplier (1x fees) is on the low end
for a contract of this size — 2x to 3x fees is more typical market position for
mission-critical SaaS at this ACV tier."
