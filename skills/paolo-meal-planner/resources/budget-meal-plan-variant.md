# Budget Variant - GBP 50 Week, 5-Day Plan (Hartley Family)

**Household:** the same family as the standard plan, 2 adults and 2 children
aged 7 and 10. **Budget:** GBP 50 per week, down from GBP 90.

**This is a 5-day plan, Monday to Friday, and the weekend is not covered.**
That is stated here rather than buried, because it changes what the total
means. Paolo confirms with the household first whether the weekend is covered
separately, at relatives' or self-catered, instead of quietly thinning seven
days to fit.

## What the Total Is, and What It Is Not

  Groceries for the 5 days (itemised below, 24 lines):  GBP 34.50
  Contingency: milk top-up, a snack:                 GBP 4.00
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  Five-day basket:                                  GBP 38.50
  Weekly budget:                                    GBP 50.00
  Left for the two uncovered weekend days:          GBP 11.50

So the honest figure is GBP 38.50 for five days, leaving GBP 11.50 of
the weekly GBP 50 for the two days this plan does not cover. It is not a
GBP 50.00 spend with GBP 11.50 of unexplained items in it. If the weekend
needs covering too, GBP 11.50 across two days for four people is tight but
workable on the same patterns: one more batch cook and one more jacket potato
night. Paolo would rather say that than present a number that does not
reconcile.

## What Changed from the GBP 90 Plan

- **Protein consolidated to one small pack of mince, plus eggs, lentils and
  beans.** The GBP 90 plan carried chicken thighs, a whole chicken and beef
  strips. This one carries 250g of mince across 4 servings, which is about 62g
  per serving. That is the real trade-off at this budget and it is better said
  plainly: the mince is a flavour base and the lentils and beans are the
  protein, which is why both appear in volume.
- **Cheapest versatile vegetables.** Cabbage and carrots replace broccoli and
  peppers. Same cross-over principle, chosen on cost per kg.
- **Two batch cooks instead of a roast.** Lentil soup and pasta bake, each
  cooked once and eaten across two days, plus frozen portions.
- **No separate prep session.** Both batch cooks happen on the evening they
  are first eaten, so no dedicated prep block is needed. Paolo does not impose
  prep infrastructure on a plan that does not need it.
- **Pizza night and the cooked breakfast are gone.** Replaced with jacket
  potatoes and porridge, which is the honest version of the swap.

## The Week

### Monday
- **Breakfast:** Porridge with a spoon of jam.
- **Lunch:** Egg and cress sandwiches.
- **Dinner:** Lentil and carrot soup with crusty bread. Cooked as a large
  batch: tonight's dinner, Tuesday's lunch, and 2 portions to the freezer.

### Tuesday
- **Breakfast:** Toast with margarine and jam.
- **Lunch:** Leftover lentil soup in a thermos. Cooked Monday, eaten Tuesday,
  1 day in the fridge.
- **Dinner:** Beef mince and onion pasta bake. 250g of mince stretched with
  tinned tomatoes and pasta. Batch cooked: tonight, Wednesday's lunch, and 1
  portion to the freezer.
- **Tonight:** move one soup portion from freezer to fridge.

### Wednesday
- **Breakfast:** Scrambled egg on toast.
- **Lunch:** Leftover pasta bake. Cooked Tuesday, eaten Wednesday, 1 day.
- **Dinner:** Lentil soup, the defrosted freezer portion, with buttered
  cabbage on the side.
- **Tonight:** move the pasta bake portion from freezer to fridge.

### Thursday
- **Breakfast:** Toast with peanut butter.
- **Lunch:** Cheese and cress sandwiches with an apple.
- **Dinner:** Pasta bake, the defrosted freezer portion, with a shredded
  cabbage and carrot salad.

### Friday
- **Breakfast:** Porridge with sliced banana.
- **Lunch:** Beans on toast.
- **Dinner:** Jacket potatoes with grated cheese and baked beans, side of
  peas.

## The Same Storage Rule Applies at Any Budget

Nothing above sits in the fridge for more than 2 days. The Food Standards
Agency advises eating leftovers within 48 hours or freezing them, so the
Wednesday and Thursday dinners are frozen portions rather than Monday's and
Tuesday's pots kept on. Each batch is split into single-meal portions before
freezing, because the FSA advises reheating food only once. Frozen portions
defrost in the fridge overnight, which is why each day names the container
that moves.

A tighter budget is a reason to waste less food, not a reason to keep it
longer. The second lentil soup portion is the cheapest meal of the week and
it is still frozen rather than held four days in the fridge.

**One note on the lentils.** Red lentils are not kidney beans and carry no
comparable lectin issue, so they need only the usual simmer until soft. The
warning about dried red kidney beans in `sunday-batch-prep-guide.md` applies
to that bean specifically. This plan uses dried red lentils and tinned baked
beans, neither of which is affected.
