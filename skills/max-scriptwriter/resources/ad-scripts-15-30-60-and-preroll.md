# Ad Scripts - One Idea at 15, 30 and 60 Seconds, Plus YouTube Pre-Roll

Invented brand: **Ledgerly**, an automatic savings and investing app. One creative idea cut
to four different clocks, so you can see what actually happens to a script when the duration
changes - which is not "say it faster".

## The word budget, and why the broadcast number is lower than the arithmetic

A normal read is about 150 words per minute, or 2.5 words per second. That gives the pure
arithmetic ceiling:

| Duration | Arithmetic ceiling at 150 wpm | Working VO budget | What the difference pays for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 37 words | 28-32 words | brand tag, one SFX beat, end card |
| 30 s | 75 words | 60-68 words | brand tag, music intro/outro, legal line |
| 60 s | 150 words | 120-135 words | two music transitions, SFX, legal line, pauses |
| 5 s pre-roll (unskippable slot) | 12 words | 8-10 words | nothing - it is all hook |

The working budget is lower than the ceiling in every row because a commercial is not a
monologue. Reserve time, not words: a 30-second spot that hits 75 words has nowhere to put
the logo sting, and a spot with a legal line has less room again because a legal line is read
at a pace the client's compliance team dictates, not at 150 wpm.

Treat the working-budget column as the professional rule of thumb it is, not a rule. Time your
own read: record the draft on a phone at the pace the talent will actually use and trust the
stopwatch over the table.

## Before you write a word: the four questions, answered for this brief

1. **Format and platform.** Broadcast radio (15/30/60) plus a YouTube pre-roll cutdown.
2. **Length.** All four, from one idea, so the campaign reads as one campaign.
3. **Speaker.** Single female VO, warm and dry rather than bright and excited. No two-voice
   dialogue - dialogue costs words and 15 seconds cannot afford a second character.
4. **What the listener should think, feel or do.** Think: *waiting is the expensive part.*
   Feel: *mild, specific regret about the years already spent waiting.* Do: download the app
   and turn on the automatic transfer.

## A note on the numbers, and on what not to write

The campaign rests on the same arithmetic as the explainer scripts in this package: at 7%
nominal compounded monthly, $200 a month for ten years from age 25, then left alone, finishes
ahead of $200 a month for thirty years from age 35 - $280,968 against $243,994, on $48,000 less
paid in.

**None of that goes in the ad.** A broadcast spot cannot carry an assumption set, and a
performance figure without its assumptions is the kind of claim that a financial regulator,
a broadcaster's clearance team, or both, will stop. So the ads carry the *shape* of the
argument - waiting costs more than it saves - and no figure at all. Where a client insists on
a number, the number goes on a landing page with its assumptions next to it, and the spot
sends people there.

Do not draft the legal line yourself. Mark it `[LEGAL - client compliance to supply exact
wording and required read duration]` and let the people who carry the liability write it.
Budget the seconds for it before you write the VO, because a legal line arriving after the
script is locked always eats the best line in the spot.

# 15-second radio spot - "Waiting"

**VO budget: 28-32 words. Written: 30. Runs 12.0 s, leaving 3.0 s for the sting and the tag.**

```
SFX:    A single clock tick, close, then silence. (1.5 s)

VO:     "You are not waiting until you have more money.
        You are waiting until it costs more." (2.0 s pause on "more")

VO:     "Ledgerly moves twenty-five dollars a week before you can spend it.
        Start this week."

SFX:    Logo sting. (1.5 s)

SUPER:  Ledgerly. [LEGAL - client compliance to supply exact wording]
```

**Why this is the 15 and not a trimmed 30.** Fifteen seconds holds one idea and one
instruction. There is no room for a problem, a mechanism and a benefit, so the mechanism
("moves twenty-five dollars a week") has to double as the benefit. The word "before" is
carrying the whole product promise and it is doing it in one syllable.

# 30-second radio spot - "The Cousin"

**VO budget: 60-68 words. Written: 68. Runs 27.2 s, leaving 2.8 s for music and the tag.**

```
MUSIC:  Sparse piano, low, under from the top. (fades under VO at 0:02)

VO:     "My cousin is twenty-six and she is waiting.
        Waiting for the raise. Waiting for the lease.
        Waiting until investing feels like something
        people like us do."

        (beat)

VO:     "Nobody told her the part that matters.
        What she puts in at twenty-six
        beats what she puts in at thirty-six.
        Not a little more."

VO:     "Ledgerly moves the money every week,
        before you can call it a bad week.
        Twenty-five dollars. This week."

MUSIC:  Up and out. (2 s)

SUPER:  Ledgerly. [LEGAL - client compliance to supply exact wording]
```

**What the extra 15 seconds bought.** Not more product. A person. The 15 holds an assertion;
the 30 can afford a character to make the assertion about, and a character is what makes a
listener supply their own version of the cousin. The product still gets one sentence.

# 60-second radio spot - "Two People"

**VO budget: 120-135 words. Written: 132. Runs 52.8 s, leaving 7.2 s for two music transitions and the tag.**

```
MUSIC:  Single sustained note, cold open, no melody yet. (2 s)

VO:     "Two people. Same job. Same money.
        Same twenty-five dollars a week they could put away and don't miss."

VO:     "One of them starts at twenty-five.
        She does it for ten years and then she stops.
        Buys a house. Has a kid. Never puts in another dollar."

        (beat - 1 s)

VO:     "The other one starts at thirty-five
        and keeps going for thirty years. Never misses a week.
        Pays in three times as much."

MUSIC:  Shifts, warmer, under. (1.5 s)

VO:     "At sixty-five, the one who stopped has more.

        She did not earn more. She did not pick better.
        She started ten years earlier, and ten years
        is worth more than twenty years of catching up."

        (beat - 1 s)

VO:     "Ledgerly moves the money before you decide whether this is a good week.
        That is the whole product.
        Twenty-five dollars, starting this week, and then you stop thinking about it."

MUSIC:  Resolve and out. (2 s)

SUPER:  Ledgerly. [LEGAL - client compliance to supply exact wording]
```

**What the extra 30 seconds bought.** The proof. The 30-second spot asserts that waiting is
expensive; the 60 can actually run the comparison out loud, and the line "the one who stopped
has more" only works because the listener has been given both people first. This is the only
one of the four that earns a beat of silence, and it takes two of them.

Note what is still absent: no figures, no percentages, no "up to". The comparison is made
entirely in relative terms, which is what lets it run without an assumption set attached.

# YouTube pre-roll - 5-second hook plus 30-second full

The 5 seconds before the skip button is a different writing problem from a 30-second spot.
Nobody chose to watch it, so it cannot open with a premise.

## The 5-second version (the part that is guaranteed to be seen)

**VO budget: 8-10 words. Written: 9. Runs 3.6 s.**

```
0:00        ON-SCREEN: "The one who stopped investing has more."
        VO: "She stopped investing ten years in. She still won."
0:03        CUT to product frame, logo visible before the skip button is live.
```

The claim has to be complete inside the 5 seconds, because for most viewers those 5 seconds
are the entire ad. Write it as a standalone unit that loses nothing if the viewer skips at
0:05 - and then write the next 25 seconds for the minority who don't.

## The full 30-second version (skippable, continues from the same 5 seconds)

**VO budget: 60-68 words total including the first 9. Written: 58. Runs 23.2 s.**

```
0:00-0:05   [as above, unchanged - do not rewrite the first 5 seconds for the long cut]

0:05        VO: "Same monthly amount. She invested for ten years, from twenty-five.
        He invested for thirty, from thirty-five."

0:12        ON-SCREEN: two simple bars, hers taller, no numbers on them.
        VO: "Hers is bigger. She paid in three times less."

0:18        VO: "Ten years of head start beat twenty extra years of paying in.
        That is not a trick, it is just how it works."

0:24        VO: "Ledgerly. Twenty-five dollars a week, moved automatically,
        starting this week."

0:29        SUPER: Ledgerly. [LEGAL - client compliance to supply exact wording]
```

**The structural rule for pre-roll:** the 5-second unit is not the beginning of the 30, it is
a complete ad that the 30 happens to extend. If you write a 30-second spot and then chop the
first 5 seconds off it as the hook, the hook will be setup - and setup is exactly what a
viewer with a live skip button refuses to sit through.

## Timing check on all four

| Spot | Working budget | Words written | Runs at 150 wpm | Slack for music, SFX, legal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s radio | 28-32 | 30 | 12.0 s | 3.0 s |
| 30 s radio | 60-68 | 68 | 27.2 s | 2.8 s |
| 60 s radio | 120-135 | 132 | 52.8 s | 7.2 s |
| 5 s pre-roll | 8-10 | 9 | 3.6 s | 1.4 s |
| 30 s pre-roll | 60-68 | 58 | 23.2 s | 6.8 s |

The 30-second pre-roll deliberately runs 6.8 seconds short of its slot. A pre-roll has to
survive the viewer's finger hovering over Skip, so it holds two beats of silence over the
bar graphic that a radio spot would fill with words.

Every row was counted, not estimated. Do the same to your own drafts before you send them:
the single most expensive mistake in ad scriptwriting is a spot that is 4 seconds long and
gets discovered in the booth with the talent on the clock.
