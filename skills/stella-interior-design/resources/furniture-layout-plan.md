# Furniture Layout Plan — 4m x 3.5m North-Facing Rental Living Room

**The scenario:** Same room as the paint plan — 4m (long wall) x 3.5m (short wall), single window centred on one 3.5m wall, door on the adjacent 4m wall (opens inward, swings across roughly 90cm), chimney breast on the wall opposite the window, dark walnut 3-seat sofa (approx. 210cm long) to be kept, no fireplace use (decorative only), rental so no wall-mounted TV bracket unless the landlord has pre-installed one.

## The layout

- **Sofa:** placed against the long wall (4m) that is opposite the door, angled to face the chimney breast — not the window. Centring seating on a window wall is a common mistake (see below); here the sofa sits with its back roughly 15cm off the wall to allow a slim console table behind it if the room is ever used for a party/overflow seating, otherwise flush.
- **TV / focal point:** freestanding TV unit against the chimney breast wall, offset to one side of the fireplace opening rather than dead-centre on it — centring a TV unit over a decorative fireplace usually forces the unit too wide for the alcove space and blocks the fireplace visually. A 120cm-wide unit sitting in the left alcove (typical chimney breast alcoves run 90–130cm) works better than a full-wall unit.
- **Armchair:** one accent armchair placed at the end of the sofa nearest the window, angled roughly 45 degrees toward the centre of the room, creating a conversation triangle with the sofa and coffee table rather than a straight "everything facing the TV" line-up.
- **Coffee table:** centred in the seating triangle, 90cm x 50cm rectangular — leave 45cm clearance between table edge and sofa front (enough to walk past comfortably, tight enough that the table doesn't float in the middle of the room).
- **Console / side table:** slim (30cm deep) table under the window, used for a lamp and small plants — window walls are otherwise dead space in most layouts, and this fills it without blocking light.
- **Rug:** see rug sizing below — placed under the coffee table and the front two-thirds of the sofa and armchair to visually bind the seating group.
- **Traffic path:** kept clear along the wall with the door, running roughly 90cm wide from door to the rest of the room — nothing (including the console table) should encroach on this path.

## Common mistakes avoided in this layout

1. **Pushing everything against the walls.** A common instinct in a rectangular room is to line all furniture up flush against every wall, which leaves a big empty gap in the middle and makes conversation awkward — you end up shouting across a void. This layout floats the sofa slightly and angles the armchair inward instead.
2. **Facing seating at the window instead of the focal point.** In a north-facing room there's no view worth prioritising, and facing a sofa at a window wall means everyone's back is to the room's actual focal point (the chimney breast). The layout above keeps the chimney breast as the visual anchor.
3. **Centring the TV unit on the fireplace.** This almost always results in an oversized, over-furnitured chimney breast wall in a room this size. Offsetting into one alcove keeps proportion.
4. **Blocking the door swing.** No furniture piece sits within the 90cm door swing arc — check this before finalising any layout in a room with an inward-opening door.
5. **Undersized rug floating in the middle.** A rug that only fits under the coffee table (common cheap-rug mistake) makes the whole seating area look smaller than it is. See sizing below.

## Furniture size guidance for this room

- **Sofa:** a 3-seater between 200–220cm is correct for this room; anything over 230cm will crowd the 4m wall once you account for door swing and the console table opposite.
- **Rug:** minimum 200cm x 300cm for this seating arrangement — the front legs of the sofa and armchair should sit on the rug, not float off it. A rug under 160cm x 230cm in a room this size will look like a bath mat.
- **Coffee table:** should be roughly two-thirds the length of the sofa (sofa 210cm → coffee table 90–140cm) and no higher than the sofa seat height, ideally 5–7cm lower.
- **TV unit:** match to alcove width minus 10cm clearance each side, not to TV screen size — a common mistake is buying a unit sized to the TV rather than the space, resulting in either a cramped fit or an oversized unit that overwhelms the alcove.
- **Armchair:** keep it visually lighter than the sofa (open arms, exposed legs) so it doesn't compete for weight in a room already anchored by a dark, substantial sofa.
