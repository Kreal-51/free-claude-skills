# Halbert - Worked Example

## The Request
"We're launching a premium ceramic cookware line. The creative concept
from our creative director is 'quiet confidence, cookware that doesn't
need to shout to be the best thing in your kitchen.' We need a hero shoot
for the launch, six product images plus two lifestyle images with a model
cooking. We have a $12k production budget, a rented kitchen studio, and
one day to shoot. Can you art direct this?"

## Halbert's Response

### Step 1: Translating the Concept into Visual Language

"Quiet confidence" is a strategic concept, not yet a visual one. Here's the
translation into concrete visual decisions this shoot will be built on:

- **Light:** soft, directional, single-source-feeling light rather than
  flat even lighting. Confidence reads as controlled, not loud, so shadow
  detail matters as much as highlight.
- **Color:** a restrained palette, the cookware's natural matte ceramic
  tones (warm stone, deep clay, muted sage) against neutral, slightly warm
  backgrounds. No competing bright colors in frame.
- **Composition:** generous negative space around the product. Confidence
  doesn't crowd the frame. Product gets room to be looked at, not
  surrounded by busy styling.
- **Subject treatment:** for the lifestyle shots, the model's expression
  and body language should read as capable and unhurried, mid-action but
  composed, not performative "excited home cook" energy.

### Step 2: Annotated Mood Board (5 references, each with a named pull)

1. **Reference A (editorial ceramics still life):** pulled for its light
   quality specifically, the way a single soft side-light creates gentle
   gradient across a matte surface without harsh specular highlight. Not
   pulled for its background color, too cool for our palette.
2. **Reference B (minimalist kitchen interior photo):** pulled for its
   negative-space composition and how much breathing room surrounds each
   object. Not pulled for its styling density in the background, ours
   needs to be sparser.
3. **Reference C (portrait of a chef, hands mid-task):** pulled
   specifically for the model's expression and hand position, focused,
   present, not smiling at camera. This is the exact energy for our
   lifestyle shots.
4. **Reference D (product color palette board, unrelated brand):** pulled
   only for the specific stone/clay/sage tonal relationship, ignore
   everything else about this reference, it's a color-only pull.
5. **Reference E (natural window light kitchen scene):** pulled for how
   directional the light falls across a countertop, this is our lighting
   setup reference for the lifestyle shots specifically, distinct from
   Reference A's studio still-life lighting reference for the six product
   shots.

### Step 3: Shot List

Ordered by lighting setup, not by importance, because every reorder that
crosses a setup boundary costs a relight. Two setups, two blocks.

**Setup A - studio still life.** 3x4 softbox, camera left at 45 degrees,
source height just above the product's top edge, feathered so the light falls
off toward the far side of the frame. White v-flat camera right at roughly a
two-stop difference from key, black flag between the v-flat and the product to
hold the shadow side down. Reference A's light quality, not its background.
Colour target frame shot at the head of the block.

| # | Subject | Framing and lens character | Styling | Crops it must serve | Safe-area note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dutch oven, lid on | Three-quarter, longer lens, minimal perspective distortion | Bare surface, nothing else in frame | 1:1, 4:5, 16:9 | Product left of centre; clean space right of frame reserved for headline |
| 2 | Dutch oven, lid off | Straight-on, same lens | As shot 1 | 1:1, 4:5 | Interior glaze must read; watch the specular on the rim |
| 3 | Skillet | Overhead flat lay, centred | Bare surface | 1:1, 4:5 | Maximum negative space; frame wide enough that 16:9 is a crop, not a reshoot |
| 4 | Skillet plus saucepan, stacked | Three-quarter, slightly above | Bare surface | 1:1, 4:5 | Shows the tonal relationship across the line, per Reference D |
| 5 | Handle and rim detail | Macro, shallow depth | Bare surface | 1:1 only | Texture shot; not required to survive a wide crop |
| 6 | Full line, grouped | Wide, longer lens, restrained spacing | Bare surface | 16:9, 1:1 | Not a catalogue arrangement; spacing is the point |

**Setup B - natural window light.** Directional light across the counter per
Reference E, negative fill opposite rather than a bounce, so shadow detail
stays. Talent direction per Reference C. Key-to-fill closer to a one-stop
difference than the product block's two, because a face carries less contrast
than a matte ceramic body. Colour target frame shot again at the head of this
block, because this is a different light source and the grade needs its own
anchor.

| # | Subject | Framing and lens character | Styling and direction | Crops it must serve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Model mid-stir | Medium, hands and cookware sharp, environment soft | Focused, unhurried, not camera-aware. Wrong take reads as "excited home cook", ask for it again quieter | 4:5, 16:9, 1:1 |
| 8 | Model setting the finished dish down | Wider, mid-motion | Same expression direction as shot 7, so the two read as one continuous moment | 4:5, 16:9 |

### Step 4: Shoot Day Schedule

Built in setup blocks with their durations stated, so the arithmetic is
visible before the day rather than discovered during it.

| Time | Block | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | Crew call, load in, build Setup A, light, colour target, test frame | 60 min |
| 10:00 | Shots 1-2 | 90 min |
| 11:30 | Shots 3-4 | 75 min |
| 12:45 | Lunch | 45 min |
| 13:30 | Shots 5-6 | 75 min |
| 14:45 | Strike A, build Setup B, talent into hair and makeup in parallel, colour target | 45 min |
| 15:30 | Shots 7-8 | 90 min |
| 17:00 | Wrap check against the shot list, back up cards to two destinations, load out | 60 min |
| 18:00 | Out | |

**Check the arithmetic:** 60 + 90 + 75 + 45 + 75 + 45 + 90 + 60 = 540 minutes,
which is 9 hours from a 09:00 call to an 18:00 out. That fits a standard
single-day booking with no overtime, and it has exactly zero slack.

**What the per-shot time actually is:** the six product shots get 90 + 75 + 75
= 240 minutes of shooting time, which is 40 minutes per final select. The two
lifestyle shots get 90 minutes, 45 minutes each, and the first twenty of those
are warm-up with the model whatever the call sheet says. Both numbers sit
inside the 30-to-45-minute band a lit product block runs at, so the day is
tight but honest. It is tight enough that losing the Setup B relight to an
overrun costs shot 8, not ten minutes off everything.

### Step 5: Photographer Brief (excerpt)

"Confidence in this shoot means restraint, not energy. Setup A is a single
3x4 softbox camera left at 45 degrees, feathered, with a white v-flat right at
about two stops down and a black flag to hold the shadow side. I want real
shadow detail and a graded shadow edge, not a hard line and not a bright even
studio wash. Product shots should feel like the object had room to breathe
before the shutter clicked, not staged into a tight arrangement, and every
product frame needs enough clean margin that the 16:9 banner crop is a crop
rather than a reshoot. For Setup B, direct the model away from
smiling-at-camera energy: mid-task focus, similar to Reference C in the board.
If a take feels 'excited home cook,' it's the wrong take for this concept, ask
for it again quieter. Shoot a colour target at the head of each setup; the two
setups have to grade into the same family and the grade needs an anchor in
each."

### Step 6: Budget, Against the Actual $12,000

| Line | Cost |
|---|---|
| Photographer day rate plus first assistant | $3,500 |
| Kitchen studio rental, full day | $1,800 |
| Talent: half day plus 12-month digital and paid-social usage | $1,400 |
| Prop stylist, half day, plus props | $1,200 |
| Food stylist, lifestyle shots only | $900 |
| Retouching, 8 images at $175 | $1,400 |
| Digital tech and tethered capture | $650 |
| Catering, transport, contingency | $1,150 |
| **Total** | **$12,000** |

**Check the arithmetic two ways.** Line sum: 3,500 + 1,800 + 1,400 + 1,200 +
900 + 1,400 + 650 + 1,150 = $12,000, which is the stated budget exactly.
Grouped check: crew and capture (photographer, digital tech, retouching)
3,500 + 650 + 1,400 = $5,550; space and talent (studio, talent) 1,800 + 1,400
= $3,200; styling (prop stylist, food stylist) 1,200 + 900 = $2,100;
overheads 1,150. 5,550 + 3,200 + 2,100 + 1,150 = $12,000. The two routes
agree.

**Every figure above is an explicitly stated assumption.** Day rates, studio
rental, usage fees and retouching prices vary by market and by who you book,
and none of these are quoted as market rates. Replace each line with an actual
quote before this table is used to commit anything. Talent usage terms in
particular are a legal question: territory, media and duration belong in a
release drafted or reviewed by the client's own counsel, not in an art
director's budget line.

### Step 7: The Tension I Am Flagging Before Shoot Day

The budget balances, which is not the same as the budget being safe. Three
things to say out loud now rather than at 16:00 on shoot day:

1. **The contingency line is $1,150 and the schedule has no slack.** Any
   overrun in Setup A comes directly out of the 45-minute relight or out of
   shots 7 and 8. If the day slips by half an hour, shot 8 is the one that
   comes off. Decide that now, not on the day, because the decision taken at
   16:00 with a model on the clock is always the expensive one.
2. **Shot 8 is the most expensive frame in the list.** It implies a styled
   table setup with linens, tableware and a finished dish, which is what the
   $900 food stylist line is carrying on its own. Simplifying shot 8 to the
   same countertop setting as shot 7 removes that dependency, releases most of
   that line back to contingency, and costs the campaign one environment
   rather than one image.
3. **Six product shots and two lifestyle shots in one day with a lighting
   change in the middle is the honest maximum.** If anyone adds a ninth
   select, it is a second day, not a tighter schedule.

### Step 8: Delivery Specification

Stated before the shoot, because a delivery spec agreed after capture is a
regrade.

- Capture raw, tethered, with a colour target frame at the head of each
  lighting setup.
- Masters delivered as 16-bit layered files in the working space named in the
  brief, retaining the grade layers so a later placement can be re-derived
  without reopening the raws.
- Web and social delivery as sRGB, long edge 2400px, one file per required
  crop per image rather than one master left for someone else to crop.
- Print conversion is not done on a guessed profile. The printer supplies
  their own current output profile and the conversion is made against it;
  until that profile arrives, print files are not delivered.
- File naming carries campaign, shot number and crop ratio, so an asset found
  loose on a drive in eight months can still be placed correctly.

### Step 9: Cross-Asset Consistency Audit, On Delivery

Run once, across the whole set, after individual selects are approved. A set
of individually-approved assets can still read as fragmented.

| Check | What is compared | Pass condition | Action if it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product body colour | The ceramic's own colour in the Setup A frames against the Setup B frames, read off the colour target in each | Within the tolerance agreed with the brand before delivery | Regrade from the target; if the grade cannot reach it, reshoot the frame rather than pushing the file further |
| Negative space | Subject-to-frame ratio across shots 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 | Visually consistent breathing room; no frame noticeably tighter than the set | Recrop from the margin the shot list reserved |
| Shadow character | Shadow edge and density across all six product frames | One light quality across the block | Regrade; a frame shot on a different modifier cannot be graded into the set and is a reshoot |
| Talent energy | Expression and body language, shot 7 against shot 8 | Reads as one continuous, composed moment, not two energies | Select a different take from the same block before considering a reshoot |
| Crop survival | Every delivered crop of every image | Subject inside the 1:1 safe area, no crop losing the reserved headline space | Recrop; this is why the margin was reserved on the shot list |

**On the colour tolerance.** Fix the number with the brand before delivery
rather than assuming one. Colour difference is measured with a defined formula
and a defined tolerance, and the figure that counts is the one the brand's own
guidelines or their printer specify for this product. Halbert's role is to
make sure a number exists, that the target frames make it measurable, and that
nobody signs off a set on "looks close enough on my screen".

### Step 10: The Reusable Direction Document

The last deliverable, and the one that stops phase two re-deriving all of the
above from scratch: the two lighting setup diagrams with their ratios, the
colour targets and the grade applied to each setup, the crop and safe-area
convention, the talent direction language that produced the right takes, the
delivery spec, and the tolerance agreed in step 9. A second shoot that matches
this one is then a matching exercise against a written record, not a second
act of interpretation.
